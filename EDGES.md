# Backtest: do "Today's edges" predict runs?

_Generated by `scripts/backtest_edges.py` — rerun it to refresh. Read-only: changes no data._

Every 2026 regular-season game is rebuilt from what was known **before** it: the real
batting orders and starters (MLB box scores), each batter's lines from months that had
already ended plus his previous-season line (the site's Round G1 math), and each starter's
score as of Opening Day. The edge is then compared with the runs that offense scored.
Starter yardstick: last season's 100+ IP starters (median 62.5, spread 27.4).

## Verdict

**What the site does with this:** since Round G2 the edges card labels each game from the starter matchup only (the part with evidence) — "Tough matchup" at 1.0 starter spreads, "Slight edge" at 0.4 — and shows lineup wRC+ as context.

- **No clear signal.** Rank correlation with runs scored: 0.018 (95% range -0.010 to 0.048).
- Offenses labelled **Strong (bats)** scored 4.47 runs; **Even** 4.38; **Strong (starter)** 4.28 — league average 4.48.
- **The starter part carries the signal:** 0.086 (0.055 to 0.115), in line with BACKTEST.md's game test. Offenses facing a much better starter score fewer runs.
- **The lineup part does not:** -0.066 (-0.096 to -0.036). Even with hindsight (each batter's full-season line) a lineup's wRC+ predicts one game's runs at only 0.035: wRC+ is adjusted for ballparks and the run environment, raw runs are not, and a lineup also faces the bullpen.

## Limits

- Batter lines are monthly, so a game sees only months that had ended (April games lean mostly on last season).
- Monthly wRC+ is combined by plate appearances — a close approximation, not FanGraphs' exact figure.
- Starter scores are frozen at Opening Day, so the starter part is slightly undersold.
- The lineup also faces the bullpen, which the edge ignores.
- 95% ranges resample whole team-months (210 of them), since a lineup's number barely changes within a month.
